PROJECT · IV — FIG. P4

The OMIM discovery pipeline.

A three-phase agentic system that proposes a candidate gene and a candidate mechanism for diseases whose mechanism is still unknown.
— abstract —

The Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man database lists thousands of distinct phenotypes, and a meaningful share — several hundred — are flagged with the same uncomfortable annotation: phenotype real, gene unknown, mechanism unknown. These are diseases whose existence has been documented well enough to earn an OMIM number, but for which the underlying causal locus has never been mapped. Each unsolved entry is, in effect, a research prompt and a question of how much can be done with what is already public. The pipeline below answers in three phases — computational scoring, deep investigation, mechanism evaluation — each rendered as its own live panel beside the explanation.

PHASE I — FIG. P4·a

Computational scoring on a 1.8M-node graph.

A biomedical knowledge graph of roughly 1.8 million genomic elements — protein-coding genes, enhancers, lncRNAs, miRNAs — connected by ~10.6 million biological edges (regulatory, physical, co-expression, mouse-orthology) is queried with the disease's HPO phenotype terms. The phenotypes are bridged through mouse-knockout ontologies to a set of seed genes, and Personalized PageRank from those seeds spreads probability outward through the graph as traveling flows of evidence. Pulsing seed nodes emit particles along edges; targets that accumulate weight emerge as Tier 1 (top 50) and Tier 2 (top 200) ranked candidates.

PHASE II — FIG. P4·b

An autonomous agent cross-checks each candidate against ten databases.

The Phase I shortlist enters deep investigation. An autonomous biological-reasoning agent subjects each candidate to quantitative cross-checks across ten or more independent data dimensions — gnomAD constraint, GTEx tissue expression, mouse phenotypes, ClinVar, DECIPHER, Hi-C contact, single-cell atlases, conservation scores, GWAS catalog, protein-protein interaction networks. Pseudogenes and weak-signal candidates are rejected outright with a strike-through and a reason badge; the agent also surfaces fresh candidates the graph itself missed entirely, by literature cross-referencing on the phenotype keywords. The strongest survivors — usually three — earn a green "advancing" tint and pass to Phase III.

PHASE III — FIG. P4·c

In-silico mechanism evaluation. One winner.

The three finalists from Phase II are each tested against six competing mechanism hypotheses — loss-of-function, gain-of-function, dominant-negative, haploinsufficiency, splicing defect, regulatory disruption — through structural prediction, expression-pattern simulation, dosage modelling and known-variant comparison. The (gene, mechanism) pair with the highest combined evidence becomes the discovery report, alongside an experimental roadmap proposing the assays that would confirm or kill the hypothesis. The animation cycles through three test diseases — Pitt-Hopkins-like → TBR1 · haploinsufficiency, STALE → EHMT1 · loss-of-function, Coffin-Siris-like → BICRA · dominant-negative.
